# Reference Idioms

Updated 16 September 2026 after the owner approved the final Word manuscript.
Use the approved wording for the matching sense, adapting tense, number, articles
and sentence fit. Do not paste whole example sentences or add unstated imagery.

An explicit general choice such as "use both wordings" permits both in suitable
contexts. "Owner kept the original in Ch.X" is a LOCAL exception for that documented
passage only, as the owner expressly clarified. It does not broaden the default.
Preserve deliberate inversions, extensions, ironic reversals and linked wordplay.
An accepted local exception remains accepted unless the owner explicitly revises it.

The Source column contains SUSPECTED originals from the earlier collection, not
verified bilingual mappings. Retrieve by these hints, then check the actual source
and scene. Do not import them into the hard glossary or match different senses just
because a Chinese string coincides. Exact names, Talent names and system templates
are not ordinary idioms to normalize. Single-wording groups omitted from this table
remain manuscript evidence, not automatically permanent rules. Undecided groups
below remain undecided; proposed resolutions are not approvals.

## Later rulings that override the sheet

- The survival idiom (一线生机): every form ("sliver of survival", "sliver of hope", "thread of hope", "thread of survival", "sliver of life", "thread of life") is now "sliver of hope".
- Group 90: both wordings become "as steady as Mount Tai". Group 14 separately records
  "as stable as Mount Tai". This cross-group conflict remains open; do not silently merge
  the groups or reverse either record. A proposed unification awaits owner approval.
