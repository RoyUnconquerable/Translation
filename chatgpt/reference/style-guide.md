# Style guide

How the English is written. Terms, names and fixed wording live in the glossary, phrase memory and the four owner references; this file holds principles only. Provenance for each principle is in decision-log.tsv (owner revisions of Ch. 1411-1419 and the 2026-09-26 rewrite). Worked pairs are in craft-examples.md.

**How to use owner revisions.** The owner's edited chapters are better than the drafts and show the owner's preferences, but they are not a ceiling and not every line is a model. Take the principle a revision shows (clearer agency, time order, a split sentence, a stock idiom), not its exact wording, and aim for professional light-novel quality beyond it. An owner's explicit term, name or fixed-wording ruling is still binding.

## 1. The book and its reader

A comic, system-driven xianxia serial told close to one protagonist. Lü Yang is blunt, sardonic and shameless; the Dao Lords and Dao Ancestors are composed. Chapters move fast, set one-line beats between longer paragraphs, and end on a hook. Readers see only the English, which should read like a professionally translated web novel: clear, quick, idiomatic, and never stiff. Lines in that voice, from owner revisions:

- "The Dao Ancestors really did have plans of their own."
- "Only one place lies beyond its reach, and because of that, the formation remains incomplete."
- *This bastard dragged me into it!*
- *As expected of a True Monarch of my Saint Sect!*

## 2. Priorities

When principles pull against each other, the higher one wins:

1. **Meaning.** Content, degree, certainty, terms and identities are fixed (editing-spec.md lists the limits).
2. **Clarity.** The reader always knows who acts, why, and in what order.
3. **Natural English.** Idiomatic words and sentence shapes a native writer would choose.
4. **Source shape.** Chinese clause order, markers, repetition and sentence boundaries are kept only when they serve 2 and 3.

**Translation, not addition.** Chinese drops subjects, links clauses by juxtaposition and marks time by aspect; English must say these things. When the relation is unambiguous, supplying it is translation: the elided actor, an implied cause or time order, a subject for a topic-comment frame, a verb for a four-character phrase, the name for an unclear 他. A relation you only infer goes in the delivery flags.

**Invention** is not allowed: no motives, explanations, images, conclusions, emotional labels, hedges or intensifiers the source lacks; no resolving a deliberate ambiguity; no filling a withheld reveal.

## 3. Agency

- **The doer is the subject and the doing is a verb.** Name the real subject of a claim, not a bare topic: "Cultivators of the Ancient Past cultivate their nature," not "The Ancient Past cultivates nature." Lead with the agent rather than an "At the cost of..." or "With..." opener.
- **Unpack nouns that hide actions.** A coined compound becomes a verb phrase ("piggybacking on another's Transcendence"); an abstract "ending of destruction" becomes a concrete fate. Named concepts such as the Dao stay nouns.
- **The person stays the subject when a body, power or status changes,** and perception and feeling stay with the experiencer. An effect may be a grammatical subject but never perceives or decides.
- **Passive only by choice,** when the patient is the topic or the agent is unknown.
- **Resupply the right subject at each turn.** Use the name when the scene turns or a nearby noun could claim the pronoun; separate two holders of one title ("the first Dao Ancestor of Fate"). Singular they for an unidentified or generic person.
- **Keep kinds of action apart:** forced movement and retreat, authority and direct rule, understanding and explanation.
- **Attach an opening phrase to its real subject.**

## 4. Cause, time and information order

- **Tell events in the order they happened,** and put cause before effect. A chain of steps reads as a sequence: reincarnated, then proved the Dao, then became a Dao Lord, then entered.
- **Make a causal step explicit when the reader would otherwise have to infer it,** with an ordinary carrier: so, because, since, that meant, that could only mean, because of that. A source marker of cause, contrast, concession or timing needs a carrier unless English order already shows the relation; then drop it. Stock signposts ("at this moment", "however" at every turn) are dropped.
- **One main verb carries the key action.** A reversal, decision or climax gets its own finite clause, usually last. A participle tail may carry a secondary result or an accompanying state ("changing the fate of the Divine Land's destruction once and for all").
- **Untangle nested clauses.** A sentence that holds a promise inside a time clause inside a claim is rebuilt in plain order: "The Dao Venerable personally promised us that when the opportunity arrived, all things would possess a sliver of hope."
- **Keep a setup before its ironic reaction,** and keep premises beside their conclusions and conditions beside their claims. Reorder within a paragraph, never past a reveal or payoff.
- **Timing jolts get their own clause** ("The next instant...", "But just then").
- **Calques.** 如果说 X, 那 Y is a contrast ("Where...", or an idiomatic "If X, Y"), not a condition. 才 is not automatically "only then". 另一边 is "meanwhile", not "on the other hand".

## 5. Sentences and paragraphs

- **One claim per sentence.** Split a sentence that carries two ideas, two actors, or a shift of focus; give the second claim its own frame ("Both had long since cast aside their emotions. In their eyes, only Transcendence mattered.").
- **Split long descriptions into concrete beats,** each with its own verb, instead of one sentence of stacked modifiers.
- **Vary length by purpose:** a long sentence builds, a short one lands. Keep one-line beats and punch lines at source brevity, landing once.
- **Branch right:** subject and verb early, modifiers after, no stacked openers.
- **Serial actions** are finite verbs sharing one subject, strongest last, unless the last is a secondary result.
- **Speech with several observations** splits into beats, with a pause before a searching question ("It's entirely external. And that status... is it one of the Dao Venerable's methods?").
- **Repetition.** A refrain, taunt or doubled word keeps its repetition ("Strange, very strange"). Clause-level anaphora may merge into one flowing sentence when that reads better.
- **One wording per key phrase** within and across chapters (phrase memory); no elegant variation for a fixed concept.
- **Paragraph boundaries** follow translation-spec.md, including owner-style lead-in merges.

## 6. Certainty and strength

- **Match hedges.** Keep every source hedge (似乎, 好像, 几乎, 仿佛, 恐怕, 未必 "may not", 或许 "might") and add none. "That could only mean" as a causal inference is not a hedge.
- **Keep degrees apart:** difficulty and impossibility, attempt and success, overlap and fusion, understanding and ability, option and obligation, all and most, assumption and fact.
- **Keep litotes** (也不是没有 is "it wasn't as if").
- **Keep a rhetorical question a question** with its force.

## 7. Voice, register and dialogue

- **Plain syntax everywhere; precise words in narration and composed speech.** Narration and composed speakers use exact standard vocabulary (viable, formidable, intends); Lü Yang and colloquial speakers use contractions, fragments and blunt or profane diction at source strength.
- **Uncontracted registers:** maxims, scripture, edicts, formal invocations and 吾 declarations. Formal Dao Lord speech is composed and precise, never faux-Victorian, and may shift to colloquial urgency when the source does. This is the one list of uncontracted registers.
- **Purpose shapes the line.** A bargainer speaks in short, flat statements; a taunt keeps its refrain; insults, threats and comic reversals stay at source strength. Spoken lines may state what the speaker plainly implies ("Stop dreaming about escaping in person").
- **Speech tags are sparse.** Never invent a gesture or tone. Dialogue is never italicized.

## 8. Thought, viewpoint and tense

- Narration is simple past; past perfect only for needed anteriority.
- **One italics test.** Inner speech in the first person or addressed to someone (marked or unmarked, including rhetorical questions and rants that follow the character's own reasoning, and quoted words introduced as thinking) is italic, in the thinker's present: present for perception, future for plans, conditional for hypotheses. A comment or exclamation with no first person stays roman, past-tense free indirect narration.
- Direct thought is mental speech with its interruptions, certainty and sarcasm, never a report of reasoning. Free indirect thought keeps its voice: interjections, rhetorical questions, litotes, and the thinker as he.
- Rewritten history uses tense to show the new established past. Reveal no later knowledge when resolving identities.

## 9. Idioms and Chinese texture

- **Classify first.** Fixed expressions (titles, quotations, verses, proverbs, panels, formulas) follow Reference_Idioms.md and phrase memory; suspected originals there are hints.
- **A dormant idiom becomes the stock English idiom** a native writer would use, never a literal gloss ("poured out", "doing whatever it takes").
- **A live image that carries meaning is kept:** a proverb whose picture is the point ("when circumstances were stronger than he was"), a measure image ("ten thousand fathoms of red dust"), a linked chain across chapters.
- Well-known sayings use their established English form ("If I do not enter hell, who will?"). Verify an allusion's logic; never complete a clipped quotation from memory.
- Keep wordplay or flag the loss. Keep system vocabulary and Daoist and Buddhist concepts, never Western fantasy terms. No translator notes.

## 10. Names, ranks and term conventions

- Pinyin with established diacritics for birth names; translate Dao titles, ranks, techniques, artifacts, realms and epithets unless the glossary gives a pinyin name. Full epithets are never clipped (the Primordial Saint, the Ancestral Dragon).
- Ordinary pronoun case for everyone; gender and pronoun timing per entities.tsv and Reference_Formatting_Rules.md.
- Standing conventions: prove, proved, proving for 证 establishing a Great Dao; nature for fundamental character, essence for substance; Spiritual Power the foundation, spiritual power the resource; status and talent lowercase in prose; the Dao Heart; Great Divine Ability for 大神通, divine ability for 神通; keep cultivation and Dao Attainment, Fate, Destiny and Fortune, True and False History distinct. The glossary governs every other term.

## 11. Numbers and typography

- Digits with commas for exact values, words for rhetorical magnitudes, countless for hyperbole. Convert li to miles by value and give the figure in digits; a round source figure takes "some".
- Contractions in speech, thought and ordinary narration, except the registers in section 7. Never 'd forms.
- Quotation marks, sounds, ellipses, the dash ban and bold 【...】 displays follow Reference_Formatting_Rules.md; titles follow Reference_Italicized_Titles.md; system text follows Reference_Talents_and_Hundred_Lives.md.

## 12. Tells to avoid

- Stock signposts: "at this moment", "at this time", "only then" as a calque, "one after another" as filler, "in his heart" as filler, a fixed default sentence for 然而, 下一秒 or 与此同时.
- "And" chains that hide a cause or contrast; two or more "and" joins in one sentence.
- Stacked adverbs and intensifiers, rare synonyms, decorative metaphors, decorative inversion.
- Participle pileups and dangling openers; noun stacks that hide the actor.
- Literal idiom followed by a gloss; elegant variation of a fixed phrase.
- Comma splices and other calqued syntax.
- A pronoun whose referent a nearby noun could claim.
- A one-chapter owner word choice treated as a general rule (it belongs in phrase memory).
