# Reference Italicized Titles

Updated 16 September 2026. The tables retain the earlier owner-approved inventory
and occurrence counts as historical evidence; counts are not a current validation claim.

Named scriptures, sutras, canons, classical texts, treatises, records and
cultivation-technique works are italic as titles. Use the specific entries below;
the explicit roman exceptions in B override generic category rules. A title's ending
alone does not establish its category. Do not create a new term or merge differently
worded titles merely because they look related.

Spells, Great Laws, formations, artifacts (including Book of a Hundred Lives),
talismans, banners and Talent names are roman as names unless a specific approved
title entry applies. Direct thought is italic, so an otherwise roman name inside
that thought is not evidence for a new italic-title rule. Do not invert the title's
italics inside an italic thought. Bold display panels remain entirely non-italic.
Spoken dialogue stays roman as speech, while a named title within it keeps title styling.

Read A or B for relevant names. C is an observational span inventory, not an approved
title list, dictionary, thought-classification rule or mandatory chapter reading.
Extracted leading articles and adjacent words are not automatically part of a title.
For a genuinely new or ambiguous title, batch the naming and formatting question
with terminology approval; do not extend an isolated edit into a general rule.
